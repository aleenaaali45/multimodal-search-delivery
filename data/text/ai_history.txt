The history of artificial intelligence dates back to the 1950s, when pioneers like Alan Turing and John McCarthy laid the foundations of the field. Turing's seminal 1950 paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" introduced the famous Turing Test, a benchmark for machine intelligence. The Dartmouth Conference in 1956, organized by McCarthy, is widely considered the birthplace of AI as a formal discipline. Early AI research focused on symbolic reasoning and rule-based systems, leading to programs that could play checkers, prove mathematical theorems, and understand simple natural language. The field went through several "AI winters" — periods of reduced funding and interest — before the deep learning revolution of the 2010s. Neural networks, particularly convolutional and transformer architectures, transformed the field. Today, large language models like GPT and Claude represent the cutting edge of what AI systems can do, capable of conversational reasoning, code generation, and complex problem solving.
