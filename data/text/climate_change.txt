Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. While climate variations are natural, scientific evidence overwhelmingly shows that human activities have been the dominant driver since the mid-20th century. The primary cause is the emission of greenhouse gases — carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide — from burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial processes. These gases trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere, raising average global temperatures by approximately 1.1 degrees Celsius since pre-industrial times. The consequences are far-reaching: melting polar ice caps, rising sea levels, more frequent extreme weather events, ocean acidification, and disruption of ecosystems. The Paris Agreement of 2015 set a goal of limiting warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius. Transitioning to renewable energy sources like solar and wind, improving energy efficiency, and protecting forests are critical strategies for mitigation.
