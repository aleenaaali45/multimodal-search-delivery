Cooking perfect pasta is both an art and a science. Start with a large pot of water — at least four quarts per pound of pasta — and bring it to a rolling boil before adding salt. The water should taste like the sea; this is your only chance to season the pasta itself. Add the pasta and stir immediately to prevent sticking. Cooking times vary by shape and brand, but always taste a minute before the package suggests. The pasta should be al dente — firm to the bite with a slight resistance at the center. Reserve a cup of starchy pasta water before draining; it's the secret weapon for emulsifying sauces. Never rinse pasta unless you're making a cold salad. Toss the drained pasta directly in the sauce over low heat, adding splashes of reserved water until you achieve a silky, clinging consistency. Classic combinations include carbonara with eggs and pancetta, cacio e pepe with pecorino and pepper, and aglio e olio with garlic and olive oil.
